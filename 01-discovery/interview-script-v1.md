## Opening (2 min, word for word)
"Hi <name>, thanks for making time. I'm <your name>, a student at KIU. We're learning how people spend their time on their own projects and ideas, the good parts and the annoying parts. We're not selling anything and we have nothing to show you, so there are no right or wrong answers. Is it okay if my teammate takes notes? We won't use your name."

## Warm-up (2 min)
1. Tell me about what a normal week looks like for you outside of classes or work.

## Core (12 to 15 min)
2. Walk me through the last time you worked on something of your own: a project, an idea, anything. What did you do, from the start?
3. Tell me about the last time something about it went badly or annoyed you. What happened?
4. What did that cost you? Time, money, anything else? How much, roughly?
5. What have you tried to fix it? Can you show me?
6. Past-tense questions for the beliefs you did NOT pick in the hypothesis (swap these to match your hypothesis, and never name the belief):
   - Tell me about the last time you asked someone for help or advice on it. What happened?
   - Tell me about the last time you stopped working on it. What led to that?
   - Tell me about the last time you chose a tool, course, or resource for it. How did that go?

## Close (2 min)
- Who else should we talk to about this?
- Could we come back when we have something to show you?
