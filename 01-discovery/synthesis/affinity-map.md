# Affinity Map

## Pattern 1

**Pattern:** Beginners mistake organizing learning resources for progress because they fear committing to the wrong stack or tutorial path.

**Interviews behind it:** 01, 05, 06

**Quotes:**
- "Honestly I spent more time making a Notion page of courses than learning anything." (01)
- "Every video says to learn something different first. One guy says React, the next says learn Java properly." (01)
- "I had the idea in September, and it's October and I still haven't written a single line." (01)
- "I started with Unity, then someone said Godot is better, so I restarted." (05)
- "I didn't want to waste months on the wrong thing." (05)
- "Every time I watch a new video I think I'm doing it wrong." (05)
- "For three days I read about which stack is more in demand." (06)
- "I started a new repo three times and all three are empty." (06)

## Pattern 2

**Pattern:** Solitary learners hit debugging dead ends and lose entire evenings because they have no immediate feedback loop.

**Interviews behind it:** 03, 04

**Quotes:**
- "I know what to learn, that's not the issue. I get an error and I can't tell what it means." (03)
- "Last Tuesday I lost three hours on one line, then found out it was a missing bracket." (03)
- "I don't have anyone to ask. My friends don't code." (03)
- "Last month I spent two evenings fixing a payment bug. That was the worst part." (04)

## Pattern 3

**Pattern:** Scoping paralysis prevents starting when learners attempt self-directed projects without clear milestone boundaries.

**Interviews behind it:** 05, 06

**Quotes:**
- "I need a project, but every idea seems either too small or too big." (06)
- "I'm not stuck exactly, I just keep changing the plan." (05)
- "In the end I found a list on Reddit and thought, let me try this one too." (06)

## Not yet a pattern

- "My biggest headache is clients changing their mind, not figuring out what to learn." (04). From a student with 2+ years experience building for relatives/clients; out of ICP.
- "It's fine, I just do it when I have time." (02). Casual hobbyist without urgency or a concrete deadline.
