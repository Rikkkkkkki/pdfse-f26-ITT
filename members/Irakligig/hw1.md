### Week 1 · Act like an owner, not a role

Although my lane is discovery, the foundation had to be set before any interview could happen, so I acted like an owner of the problem-selection step rather than waiting for a script. I contributed Problem #7 to the pool from a personally observed struggle, a friend who still had not started their first project after four days of switching between tutorials, and it scored 8/8 on the Four Filters to become our team's pick (Line 1 of `DECISIONS.md`, `owner: IG`). Hack: for Filter 2 I lined up five reachable people to talk to, my friend plus Kutaisi juniors and self-taught coders, before our interview script even existed, so discovery could start the moment the script was ready. ([`00-foundation/problem-pool.md`](../../00-foundation/problem-pool.md), [`00-foundation/four-filters-scorecard.md`](../../00-foundation/four-filters-scorecard.md), [`DECISIONS.md`](../../DECISIONS.md) line 1)

### Week 2 · Evidence over opinion

As asker on logs 03 and 04 and logger on 01 and 06, I worked to keep the surprises as evidence instead of bending them to our assumptions. Logging interview 06, I recorded the real surprise, that the grad can already code and the blocker is scoping and picking a stack, not learning, rather than forcing it to confirm our "what to learn" hypothesis. Hack: I wrote the quotes live during the call in Georgian and English side by side, so no wording was reconstructed from memory afterward. ([`01-discovery/interview-logs/06-bootcamp-tbilisi.md`](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/06-bootcamp-tbilisi.md), [`01-discovery/interview-logs/03-selftaught-kutaisi.md`](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/03-selftaught-kutaisi.md))

### Week 3 · Kill your favorite idea

I came in believing the problem was "choosing what to learn." The evidence I logged, especially interview 06 with its 9-column stack-comparison spreadsheet and three empty repos, showed that people who can already code still stall on "what to build and which stack," so our wording was too narrow and I backed marking the prediction "weakened." Hack: I checked the "we are wrong if" prediction line in each log against what people actually said, not against what we hoped to hear. ([`01-discovery/problem-hypothesis.md`](../../01-discovery/problem-hypothesis.md), [`01-discovery/interview-logs/06-bootcamp-tbilisi.md`](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/06-bootcamp-tbilisi.md))

### Week 4 · The prototype is the conversation

Instead of pitching a tool, I used the interview itself as the test. In log 04, which I asked, I had the builder pull up the salon site's actual repo with its messy early commits rather than describe his process, and while logging 06 I captured the grad showing her notes app and 9-column spreadsheet with no project started; seeing the real workaround taught more than any pitch would. Hack: I asked for a screen share so I worked from what was on the screen instead of guessing. ([`01-discovery/interview-logs/04-builder-tbilisi.md`](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/04-builder-tbilisi.md), [`01-discovery/interview-logs/06-bootcamp-tbilisi.md`](../../01-discovery/interview-logs/06-bootcamp-tbilisi.md))
